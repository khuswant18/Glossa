# User interviews: script and notes template

**Goal:** check the report's core bet, that people who read hard text in English want an explainer that *remembers*, before building Pro and team features. Aim for 10 to 15 conversations of about 25 minutes each.

## Who to talk to
- **Mostly:** people who read English at work or for study but whose first language isn't English, such as engineers reading docs, grad students reading papers, and analysts reading reports.
- **A few:** new hires in jargon-heavy companies, and language learners who read real articles.
- **Where to find them:** your X followers, LinkedIn posts, university and bootcamp Discords, r/cscareerquestions and r/GradSchool (read the rules first), and the people who leave feedback on the goodbye page.
- **Offer:** a thank-you (a coffee voucher, or early Pro free for a year).

## Before the call
- Ask them to have a recent document they struggled with ready to share.
- Don't demo Glossa until the last part. Learn their current behaviour first.

## Script

**1. Their reading (5 min)**
- What did you read in the last week that was hard to understand? Show me.
- What made it hard: words, acronyms, the subject, the English, or the length?
- How much of your week is reading like this?

**2. What they do today (8 min)**
- Last time you hit a word or phrase you didn't get, what exactly did you do? Walk me through the clicks.
- Ask about: Google, ChatGPT, a translator, a colleague, skipping it.
- How long did that take? Did it break your concentration?
- Did you trust the answer? How did you check it?
- Have you ever looked up the same word twice? What happened the second time?

**3. Memory (5 min)**
- Do you keep track of words or terms you learn anywhere (notes, Anki, a doc)? Why or why not?
- If a tool showed you, on a new page, "you looked this up last week, it meant X", would that help or annoy you?
- At work: are there company-specific terms or acronyms nobody explained to you? How did you learn them?

**4. Show Glossa (5 min)**
- Install it with them, or share your screen. Let them select something from *their own* document.
- Stay quiet and watch where they hesitate.
- What would make you keep this installed after a week?

**5. Money and wrap-up (2 min)**
- What do you pay for today that helps you read or learn?
- If the free version stayed free, what would make paying about $6/month worth it?
- Who else should I talk to?

## What to listen for
| Signal | Supports | Counts against |
|---|---|---|
| Looks up the same terms repeatedly | Word list, underlines, review | Rarely repeats lookups |
| Distrusts answers and wants proof | Page quotes, "Defined on this page" | Happy with any ChatGPT answer |
| Company jargon pain | Team glossaries (Phase 3) | No internal-term problem |
| Pastes work text into ChatGPT but worries about it | On-device mode | No privacy concern |
| Already pays for Readlang, LingQ, Migaku or Readwise | $6/month Pro | Won't pay for any reading tool |

## Notes template (one per interview)
```
Date / name / role / first language:
Hardest recent document:
Current workaround (exact steps):
Repeat lookups? (yes/no, example):
Trust / checking behaviour:
Memory habits (notes, Anki, none):
Company jargon pain (1–5):
Reaction to underlines / word list:
Would pay? For what?:
Quote worth keeping:
```

After 10 interviews, count how many show each signal in the table. If fewer than about a third repeat lookups or care about memory, revisit the plan before building Pro (see "Two decisions should be made in advance" in the report).
